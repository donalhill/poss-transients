### about
The analysis is our own code, run on the VASCO team’s public data from Brian Doherty’s VASCO-ML and VASCO-challenge repositories: their transient catalogue and its machine-learning ratings, control positions spread uniformly over each plate, and lists of observing nights and nuclear tests. Where we repeat VASCO’s results, we follow their published method and code. Asteroid positions come from JPL Horizons, star positions from Gaia, plate times from the scan headers and Palomar’s log (VizieR VI/25), calibrated against asteroid trails, and test times from the US Department of Energy report DOE/NV-209. Every number and chart on this page can be reproduced with the repository’s Makefile, from VASCO’s public files and the tables the repository holds, among them the corrected plate dates and the plate times calibrated against asteroid trails. Numbers quoted from VASCO and from other authors are taken from their papers. Watters et al. (2026, PASA) re-examined the shadow and nuclear-test results using other published lists of transients, and the VASCO team has responded (Villarroel et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.15171). Their response argues that Watters et al. used a reduced, filtered subset of transients, lacking complete timing information, too small for the shadow test, and without uncertainties. The analysis here uses the whole of VASCO’s released catalogue, within the central 6 x 6 degrees of each plate, every plate’s corrected date and time, and uncertainties from the real shadows moved onto other plates, from shifted calendars of tests, and from random catalogues built on the real data. We thank Dr Beatriz Villarroel and her colleagues for publishing their data and code; their openness made this work possible.
